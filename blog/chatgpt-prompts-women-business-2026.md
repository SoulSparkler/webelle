## Before You Start: How to Use This Guide

Each prompt in this guide uses this notation:
- `[YOUR BUSINESS TYPE]` — Replace with your business (e.g., "virtual assistant service," "vintage clothing shop," "real estate coaching")
- `[YOUR TARGET AUDIENCE]` — Replace with your ideal client (e.g., "busy moms running small businesses")
- `[SPECIFIC DETAIL]` — Replace with your specific information

Copy the prompt. Paste it into ChatGPT or Claude. Replace the brackets with your specifics. Click send.

The prompts get better when you follow up. After the first output, use: "Make this more specific" or "Add an emotional hook" or "Cut this to 100 words."

## Part 1: Marketing Prompts for Women Entrepreneurs

### Defining Your Ideal Client

```
I run [YOUR BUSINESS TYPE] for [DESCRIBE YOUR GENERAL AUDIENCE]. Help me create a detailed ideal client profile that includes: demographics (age, location, income), psychographics (values, fears, goals), where they spend time online, what they search for when they have this problem, what language they use to describe their problem, what they have already tried, and what success looks like to them. Be specific, not generic.
```

### Writing Your Unique Value Proposition

```
I help [IDEAL CLIENT] achieve [OUTCOME] without [COMMON FRUSTRATION OR SACRIFICE]. Based on this positioning, write 5 different versions of my unique value proposition: one for my website homepage, one for my Instagram bio, one for a LinkedIn headline, one for my email signature, and one for verbal introduction at a networking event.
```

### Creating Your Brand Voice

```
Analyze this example of my writing: [PASTE 3-5 SENTENCES YOU WROTE]. Based on this, describe my brand voice in 5 adjectives. Then rewrite the following generic text using my voice: [PASTE GENERIC MARKETING TEXT YOU WANT TO IMPROVE].
```

### Discovering What Your Audience Actually Searches For

```
I offer [YOUR SERVICE OR PRODUCT] to [TARGET AUDIENCE]. What are the 20 most common Google searches this audience makes when they have the problem I solve? Organize these by search intent: informational searches, comparison searches, and purchase-intent searches. Include estimated search volumes where you can.
```

## Part 2: Sales Prompts

### Writing a Sales Page That Converts

```
Write a sales page for [YOUR OFFER] priced at [PRICE]. My ideal client is [DESCRIBE CLIENT] and their biggest fear is [MAIN FEAR]. The main benefit of my offer is [PRIMARY BENEFIT]. The secondary benefits are [LIST 3-4 MORE BENEFITS]. I have [NUMBER] testimonials and [CREDENTIALS/EXPERIENCE].

Structure the page with: 1) Headline + subheadline, 2) Opening story that resonates with their problem, 3) Why they are struggling (agitate), 4) The solution (your offer), 5) What is included (features → benefits), 6) Social proof, 7) Who this is for and who it is not for, 8) FAQ addressing 5 common objections, 9) CTA. Length: 800-1200 words.
```

### Handling Objections in Sales Conversations

```
I sell [YOUR OFFER] at [PRICE]. The most common objections I hear are: 1) "I don't have the budget right now," 2) "I need to think about it," 3) "I'm not sure if I'm ready," and 4) "I can find this cheaper elsewhere." Write genuine, non-pushy responses to each objection that acknowledge the concern, provide real value or perspective, and invite them to decide in their own time.
```

### Discovery Call Scripts

```
Write a discovery call script for [YOUR SERVICE]. The call should be 30-45 minutes. Include: introduction (2 min), warm-up questions to build rapport (5 min), 8 diagnostic questions to understand their situation (15 min), bridging section where you summarize what you heard (5 min), solution presentation that directly addresses what they shared (10 min), and close with next steps (5 min). Include example responses to common objections.
```

### Following Up Without Feeling Pushy

```
Write 3 follow-up email sequences for potential clients who: 
1) Attended a discovery call but have not signed up yet (3 emails over 7 days)
2) Clicked on my sales page but did not buy (3 emails over 5 days)
3) Bought from me once but have not returned in 3 months (3 emails over 14 days)

For each sequence: Email 1 should add value, Email 2 should address the most common objection, Email 3 should be a final check-in. Keep all emails under 200 words. Warm, human tone — not desperate.
```

## Part 3: Client Communication Prompts

### Onboarding New Clients

```
Write a client onboarding sequence for [YOUR SERVICE]. Include: 1) Welcome email sent immediately after payment (warm, celebratory, sets expectations), 2) Onboarding questionnaire email (sent Day 1, asks for everything you need to start), 3) Kick-off confirmation email (sent when you receive their questionnaire, outlines next steps and timeline), 4) Check-in email (sent after 2 weeks to assess satisfaction). Keep each email under 250 words.
```

### Navigating Difficult Client Situations

```
Write a professional email responding to a client who is unhappy because [DESCRIBE THE SITUATION]. The email should: acknowledge their concern empathetically, take appropriate responsibility without over-apologizing, propose a specific resolution, set clear expectations going forward, and keep the relationship intact. Tone: calm, professional, solution-focused. Under 200 words.
```

```
Write a polite but firm email declining [DESCRIBE REQUEST FROM CLIENT] that is outside the scope of our agreement. The email should: decline clearly (not vaguely), briefly explain why without over-justifying, offer an alternative solution or path forward, and maintain a warm professional tone. Under 150 words.
```

### Requesting Testimonials

```
Write 3 testimonial request emails to send to satisfied clients of [YOUR BUSINESS]. Each version should: feel warm and personal (not automated), make it very easy to respond, explain where the testimonial will be used, offer a specific question prompt to guide their response, and include a link or clear instruction for submitting. Keep each under 150 words.
```

## Part 4: Content Creation Prompts for Women in Business

### Building a Personal Brand on LinkedIn

```
I am building my personal brand on LinkedIn as [YOUR ROLE] who helps [TARGET AUDIENCE] with [PROBLEM]. Write 5 LinkedIn posts for this week that position me as a thought leader. Post 1: a counterintuitive insight in my industry. Post 2: a personal story with a business lesson. Post 3: a practical tips list (5-7 tips). Post 4: a question to spark discussion in my audience. Post 5: a client result story (anonymized). Each post should be 200-300 words with strong first line and no generic opener.
```

### Instagram for Women Entrepreneurs

```
Write a 30-day Instagram content calendar for [YOUR BUSINESS]. Balance: 40% educational, 30% personal/relatable, 20% promotional, 10% community (polls, questions, shares). For each post, give me: the content type, the topic, the opening line, and the CTA. Format as a numbered list with the date placeholder.
```

### Pinterest for Driving Traffic to Your Website

```
Write 20 Pinterest pin descriptions for [YOUR WEBSITE] targeting [TARGET AUDIENCE]. Each pin should: start with the primary keyword, be 150-200 characters, include a clear benefit ("learn how to...", "discover...", "get the free..."), and end with a CTA. The keywords to include are: [LIST YOUR 5 MAIN KEYWORDS].
```

### Email Newsletter Content

```
Write a monthly email newsletter for [YOUR BUSINESS] subscribers. Include: 1) One personal story or observation (100 words), 2) One business insight or trend relevant to [YOUR NICHE] (150 words), 3) A practical tip or mini-tutorial (150 words), 4) A product or resource recommendation with a brief why (75 words), 5) A question or prompt to encourage replies (25 words). Total under 500 words. Warm, direct, expert tone.
```

## Part 5: Business Operations Prompts

### Writing Your Business Plan

```
Write a one-page business plan for [DESCRIBE YOUR BUSINESS IDEA]. Include: Executive Summary (50 words), Problem Statement (50 words), Solution (your offer - 75 words), Target Market (who exactly - 75 words), Revenue Model (how you charge - 75 words), Competitive Advantage (what makes you different - 50 words), Goals for Year 1 (revenue + milestones - 75 words), and Key Risks (top 3 - 75 words).
```

### Pricing Your Services

```
I offer [DESCRIBE SERVICE] and I am considering pricing it at [PRICE RANGE]. My ideal client earns approximately [CLIENT INCOME LEVEL] and is accustomed to paying for [SIMILAR SERVICES]. My competition charges [COMPETITOR PRICE RANGE]. 

Help me think through: whether my pricing is too low, too high, or appropriate; what pricing structure would work best (hourly, project, retainer, packages); how to present the price so it feels like a no-brainer to my ideal client; and what I could add or remove to justify my ideal price point.
```

### Creating Systems and SOPs

```
Write a standard operating procedure for [DESCRIBE TASK] in my [BUSINESS TYPE]. The SOP should include: Purpose (why this process exists), Trigger (what starts this process), Step-by-step instructions (numbered, with decision points noted), Tools needed, Common mistakes to avoid, and Quality check at the end. Write it so a new team member with no context could follow it.
```

### Financial Tracking

```
Create a simple monthly income and expense tracking template for a [YOUR BUSINESS TYPE] business. Include: income categories (list 5-7 relevant to my business), expense categories (list 8-10 relevant to my business), a net income calculation, a tax estimate (assume 25% for now), and a notes column. Format it as a table I can copy into a spreadsheet.
```

## Part 6: Mindset and Leadership Prompts

### Overcoming Imposter Syndrome

```
I am experiencing imposter syndrome about [SPECIFIC SITUATION — e.g., raising my prices, pitching larger clients, publishing content]. Write me a response that: acknowledges this is real and common, reframes it using evidence-based perspective, gives me 3 specific actions I can take today to build genuine confidence, and ends with a short empowering message I can save and read when I feel this way again.
```

### Preparing for Important Meetings

```
I have an important meeting with [DESCRIBE WHO] about [DESCRIBE TOPIC]. The outcome I want is [DESIRED RESULT]. The concerns they might have are [LIST CONCERNS]. The evidence I have for my position is [YOUR EVIDENCE]. Help me: prepare my key talking points, anticipate their pushback and draft responses, and write an opening that sets a collaborative tone. Also suggest 3 questions I should ask them.
```

### Setting Boundaries with Clients

```
Write a professional but warm response to a client who: [DESCRIBE BOUNDARY VIOLATION — e.g., is emailing me on weekends, asking for more than we agreed, paying late]. The response should: address the specific behavior clearly, explain my policy without apologizing for having one, provide a path forward, and maintain a positive professional relationship.
```

## 5 Power Moves to Get Better ChatGPT Outputs

### Power Move 1: Give It Your Own Examples
"Here is an example of the tone I want: [PASTE EXAMPLE]. Write this content in the same tone."

### Power Move 2: Ask It to Improve Itself
After the first output: "What is the weakest part of this response? Improve it."

### Power Move 3: Request Multiple Options
"Give me 5 different versions of this, each with a different angle or tone."

### Power Move 4: Layer In Constraints
"Rewrite this but: remove all adjectives that feel exaggerated, cut the length by 30%, and make the opening sentence a question."

### Power Move 5: Ask It to Challenge You
"What is the strongest counter-argument to the strategy I just described? What am I missing?"

## FAQ

**Q: Does the order of prompts in a conversation matter?**
A: Yes. Start conversations with context-setting (who you are, what you need, your audience). Then give the specific task. The AI remembers the conversation — so context you establish early carries through.

**Q: Can I use these prompts in Claude instead of ChatGPT?**
A: Absolutely. All prompts work in Claude, ChatGPT, and most other large language models. Claude tends to produce longer, more thoughtful responses. ChatGPT is faster for short outputs.

**Q: What do I do if the output is too generic?**
A: Add more specifics. The most common reason for generic output is a generic prompt. Add your actual business name, actual audience details, actual pricing, and actual examples.

**Q: Can I share these prompts with my team?**
A: Yes. We encourage it. AI fluency across your team makes your entire business more efficient.

**Q: How often do I need to update my prompts?**
A: As your business evolves. Revisit your saved prompts every 3 months and update them with new offers, new audiences, and new goals.
